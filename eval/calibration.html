Grade the rendered slide, not the intent text. Judge scores are hidden so they don't anchor you; reveal them only after you finish. When done, press Copy as JSON and paste the result into eval/calibration/<your-name>.json. These become few-shot examples and tuning targets for the judge prompts.

Score anchors
claim_at_rest
  1. No claim, a contradictory claim, or the slide is unreadable at rest.
  2. Right topic, wrong or missing point: the reader names the subject but not the assertion, or reads a weaker / different claim.
  3. Same core assertion as intended; a secondary nuance or qualifier is lost.
  4. The claim read from the resting slide states the intended claim's full assertion — including its causal or relational part — with nothing important missing or added.
mechanism_shown
  1. The mechanism is absent, or only named in a sentence.
  2. Mostly text. Any figure is decorative, or just lays out labelled boxes / components without showing how one drives the other. Tidy boxes-with-words are a 2.
  3. The mechanism is depicted with real visual structure (axes, arrows, flows, linked states); one link is only stated in text.
  4. The mechanism is DRAWN: a viewer can trace inputs → relation → output in the figure itself, without needing the sentences.
device_works
  1. No control, a control that does nothing, or it breaks the slide.
  2. Something responds, but not the planned change: the effect is cosmetic (a highlight, a label swap), hard to see, or the 'aha' is not reachable.
  3. Works: the planned change happens and is visible. Minor issues — weak affordance, small or subtle change, only part of the planned range.
  4. Doing the planned operation produces the planned change immediately and legibly; the control is discoverable at rest; the 'aha' is reachable by a first-time viewer.
fidelity
  1. The core claim contradicts the source, or there are multiple fabrications.
  2. At least one fabricated number, name, quote or claim, OR a material distortion of the source. (Any fabrication caps the score at 2.)
  3. Faithful. Minor simplification or paraphrase the source's author would accept; any illustrative values are clearly marked as illustrative.
  4. Every number, name, quote and claim on the slide traces to the source; framing is faithful, nothing distorted.
craft
  1. Broken: overlapping or cut-off text, illegible, chaotic.
  2. Tidy but flat (everything the same weight), or large dead zones, cramped clusters, small/low-contrast text, awkward alignment.
  3. Clear hierarchy and balanced layout; at most one minor issue (a cramped corner, one small label).
  4. A clear single entry point and deliberate hierarchy; space is used on purpose; nothing cramped, tiny, or stranded. Would hold up printed as a poster.
not_generic
  1. A template slide: 3+ defaults, content interchangeable.
  2. The layout is defined by a default (card grid, hero tagline, stat tiles), or 2+ named defaults are present.
  3. At most one minor default (e.g. a small eyebrow label) that does not define the layout.
  4. No named defaults. The visual form is specific to this idea — you could not swap in another topic's text.

Two separate jobs

dont-scale · s_3esimwaa · instrument
claim
Starting a startup and keeping it running are two separate jobs. The first one is manual, and it ends at a threshold.
mechanism
Cranking adds RPM. Below the ignition line, RPM decays back to zero as soon as cranking stops. Above it, the engine sustains its own RPM and the crank is no longer needed. The input is the founder's effort. The relation is the threshold. The output is self-sustaining motion, or a fall back to cold.
operate
The reader turns the crank (presses or drags to add turns) and can let go at any moment.
changes
The tachometer needle rises with each turn. If released below the ignition line, it sinks back to cold. Once past the line, the engine catches, the crank arm drops away, and the needle holds steady in the 'running on its own' zone.
aha
Letting go just short of the line sends the needle all the way back to zero. Crossing it makes the arm unnecessary. These are two different jobs, and the first one doesn't partly count.

Compounding: small numbers aren't small

dont-scale · s_aewjm1n7 · instrument
claim
At 10% a week, 100 users become about 14,000 in a year and about 2 million in two. Small early numbers are the start of that curve.
mechanism
Users after w weeks = 100 × (1 + r)^w. The weekly rate r and the elapsed weeks w are the inputs. Each week's gain is a percentage of an ever-larger base, so the increments look trivial at first and then explode. The output is the user count over time.
operate
A weekly growth-rate slider (1% to 10%) and a time scrubber (week 0 to week 104).
changes
The user curve redraws and a counter shows users at the scrubbed week. Week 1 at 10% reads 110, barely different from 100. Scrubbing to week 52 shows about 14,000 and to week 104 about 2 million. Lowering the rate to 5% collapses the two-year figure to about 16,000.
aha
Going from 100 to 110 looks like nothing, and the curve looks flat for months. Then the same 10% produces 2 million. The 10 users you recruit by hand are the first steps of that curve.

Why It Has to Be Unscalable

dont-scale-committed-plan · s_0p7uw7ur · comparison
claim
Scalable tactics assume a force already in motion; at the start none exists, so disproportionate personal effort is the only lever available.
mechanism
Two levers acting on the same load: the 'scalable' lever (word-of-mouth, brand trust) requires pre-existing momentum to transmit any force, so at zero momentum it moves nothing; the 'unscalable' lever (direct personal effort) supplies force from the founder directly and works precisely because the user count is tiny.
changes
At zero-momentum the scalable lever is slack and transmits no force; the unscalable lever is taut and moves the load — the difference is whether the lever needs a crowd that doesn't exist yet.

mechanism
A projectile gets one impulse (the big launch or a big partnership) and then only decays, coming down whatever the starting speed. A powered aircraft keeps generating thrust: a core of happy users whose enthusiasm keeps driving growth. The inputs are initial impulse versus sustained thrust. operate

changes
With the engine off, a bigger launch makes the arc taller, but it always lands back at zero. With the engine on, even a tiny launch climbs and keeps climbing. The two flight paths draw over the same timeline.
aha
Pushing the launch slider to maximum never keeps the cannonball up. Turning the engine on keeps even the smallest launch flying.
